# Natural writing

Remove accidental AI patterns without flattening or fabricating the writer's
voice.

- Match an available voice sample before applying generic preferences.
- Prefer contractions and plain verbs when the sample supports them. Do not
  manufacture casualness.
- Preserve deliberate rhythm, warmth, humor, emphasis, and informality. Let the
  prose take a position only when the source material or authorial intent
  supports it. Do not invent personal experience or a stance for the user.
- When a firsthand incident is available, prefer it to a generic thesis-first
  opening. Follow its real shape and use only the actor, constraint, action,
  result, or caveat that the source provides.
- Prefer specific observations to generic abstractions. Do not invent details to
  make the prose seem specific.
- Let the content determine the structure. Do not force a contrast, reversal,
  range, three-part rhythm or list, or extended metaphor.
- Treat polished maxims and abstract directives as suspect. Keep them only when
  the writer would say them and they add specific meaning.
- Repeat a term when that is clearer than cycling through synonyms.
- Remove repetition only when it adds no meaning or emphasis.
- Remove chatbot ceremony, reflexive agreement or praise, vague attribution,
  empty promotional language, and generic conclusions.
- Preserve functional labels, standard headings, exact names, and useful domain
  or search terms. Not every terse line needs personality.
- On the final pass, apply both semantic tests:
  - Portability: could this fit unchanged in an unrelated professional post?
    Make it specific or cut it.
  - Spoken language: would the writer say this while explaining the situation to
    a coworker? Rewrite it without flattening the voice.
- Preserve personality unless it makes the meaning unclear.
